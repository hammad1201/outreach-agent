How it should read: like a real person dashing off a quick note to someone they'd like to help. Plain words, short sentences, contractions. Something you'd write on your phone in a minute, not a marketing email. If a sentence sounds like it came from a template, a brochure or a LinkedIn post, cut it.

`email1`: 40–90 words, at most four short paragraphs.
- Start with the greeting: {greeting}
- Name {company} once, naturally, in the email itself (the subject doesn't count).
- One or two sentences on the specific thing you noticed and why it caught your eye. Put it in your own words, the way you'd mention it in conversation. Don't quote their site or job ad in quotation marks.
- Say who you are in one plain sentence that names {business} and what it does for businesses like theirs, the way you'd introduce yourself to someone at an event. One sentence, no adjectives like "cutting-edge" or "leading".
- One sentence on what you'd do about their specific situation, and if it fits naturally, one plain line of relevant experience from the facts.
- End with one simple, low-pressure question, e.g. "Worth a quick chat?" or "Is this on your radar at all?"

`followup1` (about 4 days later): one or two sentences, under 40 words. Add one small new thing: a relevant example or a quick idea. End with a light question.

`followup2` (about 10 days later, the last one): one sentence, under 30 words. A friendly close, no guilt trip.

`subject`: 2–5 lowercase words, like a note between colleagues, e.g. "bookings at harbour cuts" or "app for the trailcam". No "Re:", no punctuation tricks.

- describe {business} in brochure terms ("end-to-end", "full-stack", "solutions", "cutting-edge") or spend more than one sentence on it

- pitch a "free 30-minute discovery call" — just ask if they'd like to talk
- use dashes (— or –), semicolons, bullet points or exclamation marks
